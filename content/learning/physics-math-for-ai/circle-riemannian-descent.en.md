## Optimize while staying on a manifold

Minimize f(x)=−x₁ on the unit circle S¹. The minimum is at (1,0), while (−1,0) is a maximum. An unconstrained ambient step need not remain on the circle. Riemannian optimization first represents the differential in the tangent space and then maps a tangent step back to the manifold.

The metric here is induced by the ambient Euclidean inner product. It is not a Fisher metric merely because both methods use geometric language.

## Project the ambient gradient

At x=(cos θ,sin θ), tangent vectors satisfy xᵀv=0. Projecting the ambient gradient −e₁ gives

$$
\operatorname{grad}_{S^1}f=(I-xx^T)(-e_1)
=(-\sin^2\theta,\cos\theta\sin\theta),
\qquad \|\operatorname{grad}f\|=|\sin\theta|.
$$

The orthogonality residual in the readout checks tangency. In angle coordinates the negative-gradient velocity is θ̇=−sin θ. Objective excess is 1−cos θ, evaluated with a stable half-angle formula near the minimum.

## Two valid returns to the circle

For tangent v=−ηgrad f, normalization gives the retraction Rₓ(v)=(x+v)/‖x+v‖. Since xᵀv=0, its denominator is √(1+‖v‖²), so it never divides by zero. The corresponding angle update is θ⁺=θ−atan(η sin θ).

The circle exponential follows the geodesic generated by that same tangent vector, giving θ⁺=θ−η sin θ. It is the exact geodesic step, not the exact solution of the nonlinear gradient-flow ODE for a whole time interval. Retraction and exponential agree locally but differ at finite steps.

From θ=π/2,η=0.5, normalization gives (1/√5,2/√5)≈(0.447214,0.894427). The exponential gives (sin0.5,cos0.5)≈(0.479426,0.877583). Both satisfy the constraint, yet their endpoint distance is approximately 0.036350.

## Stationarity does not certify a minimum

At θ=π, tangent gradient is exactly zero, so either update stays at the antipodal maximum. Objective excess remains two. The implementation treats this known exact angle as an exact stationary point rather than turning floating-point sin π noise into a fictitious escape.

A tiny tangent residual can therefore identify a maximum as easily as a minimum. Curvature, descent behavior and the objective value carry additional information. General manifold problems can have many stationary points and require problem-specific convergence assumptions.

## Prove decrease for a finite geometric step

In angle coordinates F(θ)=−cos θ and |F″|≤1 everywhere. Taylor's upper bound for the exponential update therefore gives

$$
F(\theta-\eta\sin\theta)\le F(\theta)
-\eta(1-\eta/2)\sin^2\theta.
$$

For 0<η<2, each nonstationary exponential step strictly decreases this particular objective. Summing proves Σsin²θₖ<∞ and hence sin θₖ→0. It proves approach to stationarity; initialization exactly at the maximum is still a counterexample to an unconditional minimum claim.

For normalization retraction write the same update with effective step a(θ)=atan(ηsin θ)/sin θ, using limit η when sin θ=0. For nonzero sine, atan z has the same sign as z and magnitude at most |z|, so 0<a≤η. The same bound applies with a in place of η. At fixed η>0 the effective step also has a positive lower bound over the circle. Consequently 0<η<2 gives the same stationarity conclusion. Endpoint η=2 needs separate treatment and is not covered by the strictly positive decrease coefficient used here.

| θ | F″(θ) | Gradient norm | Excess |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| π/2 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| π | −1 | 0 | 2 |

```python
from math import sin, cos, atan
eta = 0.5
for mode in ("exponential", "normalization"):
    for initial in (-2., .8, 2.8):
        theta = initial
        for _ in range(20):
            sine = sin(theta)
            effective = eta if sine == 0 else (
                eta if mode == "exponential" else atan(eta*sine)/sine)
            next_theta = theta-effective*sine
            upper = -cos(theta)-effective*(1-effective/2)*sine*sine
            assert -cos(next_theta) <= upper+1e-14
            theta = next_theta
        print(mode, initial, "final excess:", 2*sin(theta/2)**2)
```

The code checks actual twenty-step paths at three starts for both returns. The proof uses this circle objective's global curvature bound, not a generic Euclidean smoothness assertion for every manifold or retraction. Near the minimum the linearized angle factor is 1−η; near the maximum it is 1+η, while the exact maximum remains stationary.
