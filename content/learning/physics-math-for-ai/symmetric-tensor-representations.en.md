## Two indices create a different representation

A vector transforms by v'=gv. A rank-two tensor has an action on both indices. In orthonormal Euclidean coordinates, this symmetric-tensor example uses the active action T'=gTgᵀ. For a quadratic form it ensures (gv)ᵀT'(gv)=vᵀTv. A passive coordinate change would use a different convention and should not be mixed into the same formula without tracking inverse maps and index types.

The controls specify an arbitrary real symmetric matrix T=[[p,q],[q,r]]. It need not be positive semidefinite, so not every setting is a covariance matrix. Its quadratic form is px²+2qxy+ry²; the off-diagonal coefficient contributes twice.

## Separate scalar and traceless parts

Every symmetric two-by-two tensor has a unique decomposition

$$
T=sI+\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
s=\frac{p+r}{2},\quad a=\frac{p-r}{2},\quad b=q.
$$

The trace part s is invariant under orthogonal conjugation. Directly multiplying RθTRθᵀ gives a double-angle action on the traceless pair:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}a'\\b'\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&-\sin2\theta\\
\sin2\theta&\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A horizontal reflection first sends b to −b, then the rotation applies. The implementation compares the actual tensor matrix product with that predicted coefficient law. Tiny residual differences are reported as floating-point discrepancies rather than being used to alter the transformation rule.

## Read the coefficient diagram correctly

Blue and orange vectors lie in the (a,b) coefficient plane. They are not physical position vectors. For T=diag(2,0), s=1 and (a,b)=(1,0). A physical rotation by π/4 produces tensor [[1,1],[1,1]], whose pair is (0,1): a quarter turn in coefficient space. A physical π/2 rotation gives diag(0,2), with pair (−1,0).

Trace, determinant and eigenvalues remain fixed. Eigenvalues are s±√(a²+b²), making the PSD condition s≥√(a²+b²). For T=I the traceless pair vanishes and rotating the tensor changes nothing.

## Irreducibility depends on the group

Under continuous SO(2), the nonzero-weight double-angle plane is a real irreducible representation. Restrict to C4 generated by physical π/2 rotations: that plane now sees only −I as its generator. Every line is invariant, so the restriction becomes two copies of the alternating one-dimensional character. This differs from the irreducible single-angle C4 plane in the earlier unit.

## Exercise and explained answer

Set T=[[1,1],[1,−1]], θ=0 and enable reflection. The scalar is zero and the pair (1,1) becomes (1,−1); trace zero and determinant −2 stay fixed. Explain why this symmetric tensor cannot be a covariance: its eigenvalues are ±√2. Then choose isotropic identity and verify invariance at every angle.

## Connection to scientific AI

Covariances, stresses, diffusion tensors and higher-order neural features require transformation laws appropriate to their indices. Treating all channels as unrelated scalars discards those laws. Specify tensor order, symmetry, metric conventions and positivity constraints before designing equivariant layers or interpreting learned tensor fields.
