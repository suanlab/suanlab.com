## Separate an algebra from a conservation law

Two observables can both be conserved and still have a nonzero Poisson bracket with each other. This sounds contradictory only if a bracket is interpreted as a general test for whether quantities change. Time change is generated by the particular Hamiltonian; brackets between other functions answer different questions.

We use three-dimensional angular momentum to make the distinction concrete. The same angular-momentum functions are evaluated under isotropic, partially symmetric and anisotropic oscillator Hamiltonians. Their mutual bracket algebra stays the same while their conservation properties change. Actual trajectories come from three independent analytic Cartesian oscillator or free-particle flows, not a new three-dimensional numerical integrator.

## Specify a full Cartesian phase space

Let position be x=(x,y,z), momentum p=(px,py,pz), and mass m>0. These are three canonical pairs. Our convention is

$$
\{F,G\}=\sum_{j=1}^3
\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial G}{\partial p_j}
-\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_j}\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_j}\right).
$$

All three position components have units m and all three momentum components kg m/s. An angular-momentum component has units kg m²/s, or Js. Therefore a bracket of two angular momenta also has units Js. A bracket of angular momentum with an energy has units J, interpreted here as torque N m because angle is dimensionless in SI.

These unit matches permit comparison of the three angular components on a common axis. They do not permit drawing a norm that mixes position and momentum. A plot of (x,y), (y,z) or (z,x) is an explicitly labeled spatial projection, not a full phase portrait.

## Differentiate the angular-momentum functions

Define L=x cross p, so

$$
L_x=yp_z-zp_y,\quad
L_y=zp_x-xp_z,\quad
L_z=xp_y-yp_x.
$$

For Lx, the position gradient is (0,pz,−py), while the momentum gradient is (0,−z,y). In vector notation, with e_i the selected Cartesian unit vector,

$$
\nabla_xL_i=p\times e_i,\qquad
\nabla_pL_i=e_i\times x.
$$

Differentiate the functions on the full six-dimensional phase space before substituting a selected state. A trajectory whose z and pz happen to be zero does not erase those derivatives from the original three-dimensional observable functions.

## Derive one bracket and obtain the cyclic algebra

Insert the gradients of Lx and Ly into the canonical definition. The surviving terms combine to xp_y−yp_x, giving

$$
\{L_x,L_y\}=L_z,\quad
\{L_y,L_z\}=L_x,\quad
\{L_z,L_x\}=L_y.
$$

Antisymmetry supplies the reversed pairs. The full matrix is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
0&L_z&-L_y\\
-L_z&0&L_x\\
L_y&-L_x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This matrix depends on the phase point through L. At a phase point with L=0, every displayed entry is zero. That does not make the underlying algebra identically zero as a function on phase space. Likewise, none of these equations yet specifies whether L changes with physical time: the Hamiltonian has not entered this derivation.

## Choose a Hamiltonian and recover torque

Take independent nonnegative stiffnesses:

$$
H=\frac{p_x^2+p_y^2+p_z^2}{2m}
+\frac{k_xx^2+k_yy^2+k_zz^2}{2}.
$$

The physical force is (−kx x,−ky y,−kz z), and the bracket-generated angular rates are

$$
\dot L_x=\{L_x,H\}=(k_y-k_z)yz,
$$
$$
\dot L_y=(k_z-k_x)zx,\qquad
\dot L_z=(k_x-k_y)xy.
$$

Directly differentiating x cross p gives velocity cross p plus x cross force. The first contribution vanishes for p=m velocity, so the result is exactly the torque. This provides a second route to the same rate, independently of the mutual L brackets.

Positive stiffness does not imply angular-momentum conservation. An anisotropic restoring force generally fails to point along the position vector. Its torque can rotate or change the angular-momentum vector while conserving the total mechanical energy.

## Compare globally conserved components

If ky=kz, the expression for Lxdot vanishes at every phase point: Lx is globally conserved for this Hamiltonian. Similarly, Ly requires kz=kx and Lz requires kx=ky. Full isotropy conserves all three components.

The model also identifies a weaker, orbit-specific condition. If one of the two coordinates multiplying a torque remains identically zero, that torque component vanishes along the selected orbit even when its stiffness coefficient is nonzero. For these uncoupled analytic equations, a coordinate stays identically zero exactly when its initial position and velocity are both zero.

An instantaneous zero torque is weaker still. A coordinate can be zero at the current cursor position and become nonzero immediately afterward. The interface must distinguish a global conservation identity, constant value on the selected orbit and a zero at one time.

## Work through conserved but noncommuting quantities

Choose m=1 kg, equal frequency codes f=(2,2,2), initial position (1,1,1) m and initial velocity (1,0,1) m/s. Initially p=(1,0,1) kg m/s and

$$
L=(1,0,-1)\ \mathrm{Js}.
$$

All three angular components remain constant because the potential is isotropic. Nevertheless, {Lx,Ly}=Lz=−1 Js throughout that trajectory. Conservation tests the brackets with H, not whether every pair of conserved observables commutes.

The selected frequency code gives Omega=pi/2 per second on each axis. Equal frequencies ensure that the entire trajectory lies in the plane spanned by its initial position and velocity, but we still evaluate the full three-dimensional observable algebra. A convenient physical plane does not turn Lx and Ly into independent planar canonical momenta.

## Start with zero angular momentum and nonzero torque

Choose frequency codes (0,1,2), m=1 kg, initial position (1,1,1) m and zero initial velocity. Then all angular momenta and their mutual bracket matrix are zero initially. The three stiffnesses are 0, pi²/16 and pi²/4 N/m. The initial torque is

$$
\tau=\left(-\frac{3\pi^2}{16},\frac{\pi^2}{4},-\frac{\pi^2}{16}\right)
\ \mathrm{N\,m}.
$$

Thus angular momentum begins changing immediately. Its initially zero value and initially zero mutual bracket matrix do not establish conservation. Total energy is 5pi²/32 J and remains conserved despite the angular change.

For a partial-symmetry comparison, use codes (4,1,1). Lx is globally conserved because ky=kz, but the other components generally change. An all-or-nothing label such as “angular momentum conserved” would hide this component-specific structure.

## Detect the planar restriction trap

Use initial position (1,0,0) m and velocity (0,1,0) m/s, with frequency codes (1,2,0). The motion stays in the z=0 plane. Along it Lx=Ly=0 and Lz initially equals 1 Js. Initial torque about z is zero because y=0, but y soon changes and kx differs from ky, so Lz does not remain constant.

On the full three-dimensional phase space, {Lx,Ly}=Lz still equals 1 Js at the initial state. If z=pz=0 were substituted into Lx and Ly before differentiating, both restricted functions would become zero and their two-dimensional bracket would also be zero. Those are different operations on different phase spaces.

There is a geometric reason for the mismatch: rotations generated by Lx or Ly generally move a state out of the chosen plane. After removing z and pz, those functions cannot retain their full role as generators of spatial rotations. Lz generates rotations within the plane.

Use the correct reduced variables and reduced bracket when formulating a constrained theory. This lesson flags the mismatch; it does not claim to develop the general reduction procedure or Dirac-bracket theory.

## See angular momentum generate rotations

For a small dimensionless angle parameter epsilon about axis i,

$$
\delta x=\epsilon\{x,L_i\}=\epsilon(e_i\times x),\qquad
\delta p=\epsilon\{p,L_i\}=\epsilon(e_i\times p).
$$

At x=(1,0,0) and p=(0,1,0), a rotation about z gives position change direction (0,1,0) and momentum change direction (−1,0,0). These are variations per unit angle, not physical velocities or forces. A rotation can be generated whether or not it preserves a particular anisotropic Hamiltonian.

A nontrivial Jacobi check uses (x,Lx,Ly). Its cyclic terms are {x,Lz}=−y, {Lx,−z}=+y and zero. They cancel with length units. This is a worked check of signs and derivatives, not a proof of the identity for every observable.

## Read the controls and numerical evidence

The controls choose mass, three frequency codes, an initial-position preset, an initial-velocity preset, the displayed rotation axis and physical query time. Position presets are (1,0,0), (1,1,1), (1,−1,0.5), (0,0,0), and (−1,0.5,1) m. Velocity presets are (0,1,0), (1,0,1), (−1,1,0.5), (0,0,0), and (0.5,−1,1) m/s.

Each stiffness satisfies ki=m(pi fi/4)². Changing mass at fixed frequency codes therefore changes stiffness too: the spatial trajectory stays the same while momentum, angular momentum, energy and torque scale with mass.

The model evaluates 201 times over two seconds. Bracket, torque and energy residuals remain numerical values rather than being replaced by zero. Their maxima are sampled diagnostics, not certified continuous-time bounds. Independent symbolic gradients and 60-digit trajectory derivatives check the formulas. Spatial projections explicitly retain which pair of axes is displayed; a projected crossing does not mean a full phase-space trajectory intersects itself.

## Exercises with explanations

1. Derive {Lx,Ly} from the coordinate gradients. **Solution:** Expand the three canonical pairs before substituting a state. The remaining terms are xp_y−yp_x=Lz. Reversing the arguments gives −Lz.
2. Can Lx and Ly both be conserved if their bracket is nonzero? **Solution:** Yes. Isotropy gives {Lx,H}={Ly,H}=0, while their mutual bracket equals Lz, which can be nonzero. These equations answer different questions.
3. Which component is globally conserved for kx different from ky=kz? **Solution:** Lx, because its torque coefficient ky−kz vanishes identically. Other components may still be constant on exceptional selected orbits.
4. Why does zero initial L not imply zero initial angular rate? **Solution:** In the (0,1,2) frequency example, zero initial momentum makes L zero, but position cross anisotropic force is nonzero. The displayed initial torque predicts immediate change.
5. Explain the planar bracket discrepancy. **Solution:** Restricting Lx and Ly to z=pz=0 before taking derivatives gives different functions on a smaller phase space. It cannot be used to disprove the full six-dimensional algebra evaluated afterward.
6. Is {x,Lz} a physical velocity? **Solution:** It is a position variation per unit rotation parameter. Physical velocity is {x,H}; the generator and its units determine the interpretation.

## Research connection and further reading

A learned dynamics model should distinguish representation algebra, a Hamiltonian's symmetries and conservation along selected data. Isotropic training trajectories alone cannot establish a proposed conservation law for anisotropic dynamics. Inspect interventions on stiffness and initial conditions, and compare angular rates to physical torque rather than relying only on conserved energy.

For further discussion of angular-momentum brackets and their generator interpretation, see David Tong's [Hamiltonian Formalism, sections 4.3.1 and 4.4.1](https://davidtong.org/teaching/classical-dynamics/dynhtml/S4).
