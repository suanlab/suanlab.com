## 1. Combining systems creates pairs of possibilities

Suppose one device has two basis states and another has three. A description of the combined device needs a coordinate for every pair: six coordinates. The relevant space is a tensor product. A direct sum would have five coordinates and would describe a choice of sector, such as “use the first device or use the second.” These are different modeling decisions before any calculation begins.

Think of the coefficient array as a table with Alice's basis labels on rows and Bob's on columns. A product state fills this table by multiplying one column of Alice coefficients by one row of Bob coefficients. A general state can require a sum of such tables. Entanglement asks whether one product table suffices for a pure state; an interesting pattern of numbers alone is not a definition.

| Construction | Dimension | What its coordinates describe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct sum of dimensions 2 and 3 | 5 | Components in alternative sectors |
| Tensor product of dimensions 2 and 3 | 6 | All pairs of subsystem basis labels |
| One product vector in that tensor product | Still a vector in dimension 6 | A coefficient table that factors into two vectors |

The conceptual picture is **two local spaces → pair-indexed amplitude table → local measurement laws → reduced spectra → pure-state entanglement**. Start with finite complex inner-product spaces, normalized state vectors and ideal projective measurements. No physical time or Hamiltonian is specified in this chapter.

## 2. Bilinearity comes before a coordinate formula

A tensor product is a vector space generated by symbols u⊗v subject to linearity in each slot. Its defining property says that every bilinear map from the two original spaces factors uniquely through a linear map on their tensor product. This property makes the construction independent of the particular coordinate array used to represent it.

$$
(\alpha u+\beta w)\otimes v=\alpha(u\otimes v)+\beta(w\otimes v),\qquad
u\otimes(\alpha v+\beta z)=\alpha(u\otimes v)+\beta(u\otimes z),\qquad
\dim(\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B)=d_A d_B.
$$

The inner product of product vectors is the product of the local inner products and then extends sesquilinearly. Consequently, products of local orthonormal basis vectors form an orthonormal basis of the combined space. This proves the dimension formula rather than treating it as an array convention.

Product factors are not unique representatives: multiplying u by a nonzero complex α and v by α⁻¹ changes neither u⊗v nor its coefficient table. If both factors are normalized, opposite phases still cancel. A common phase of the entire normalized state changes its vector representative but no density operator or measurement probability. Relative phases between different table entries can change physical predictions.

## 3. Fix the pair ordering before applying operators

Let C be an unnormalized dA by dB complex matrix and N its squared Frobenius norm. We use A-major order: Bob's index changes fastest. A two-by-three table therefore becomes the list C00,C01,C02,C10,C11,C12. This flattening is linear, without a complex conjugation.

$$
|\psi\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=0}^{d_A-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_B-1}C_{ij}|i\rangle_A\otimes|j\rangle_B,\qquad
N=\sum_{ij}|C_{ij}|^2,\qquad k=i d_B+j,\qquad
C' = A C B^T\quad\text{for }(A\otimes B)|\psi\rangle.
$$

To verify the last identity, collect the coefficient of |i⟩⊗|j⟩ after the operator acts: it is the sum of Aik Ckl Bjl. The transpose of B appears because its output index j becomes the table's column index. A dagger at this position would conjugate B and implement a different operation.

Swapping the two subsystems sends |i⟩A⊗|j⟩B to |j⟩B⊗|i⟩A and transposes the coefficient table. Its coordinate permutation depends on both dimensions. Transposing a rectangular table is not the same as leaving the flattened list untouched. Before comparing two libraries' tensor outputs, check their subsystem order and flattening conventions.

## 4. Partial trace preserves every local prediction

Form the density operator ρ=|ψ⟩⟨ψ|. Alice's reduced state is the operator that reproduces the expectation of every Alice-only observable. Expanding the global trace in a product basis identifies its entries uniquely.

$$
(\rho_A)_{ik}=\sum_j\rho_{ij,kj}=\frac{(CC^\dagger)_{ik}}N,\qquad
(\rho_B)_{jl}=\sum_i\rho_{ij,il}=\frac{(C^T\overline C)_{jl}}N,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(A\otimes I))=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A A).
$$

The repeated index belongs to the subsystem being discarded. Summing all entries of a block, or merely removing selected rows, does not implement partial trace. The result has trace one. Positivity follows because each local quadratic form is a sum of global nonnegative quadratic forms, evaluated on product vectors with the discarded basis states.

The operation is independent of the orthonormal basis used on the discarded subsystem: inserting a different complete basis still inserts the same identity operator. Its matrix representation on the retained subsystem changes covariantly under a retained-basis change. Thus “basis independent” does not mean that the displayed matrix entries never change.

Partial trace discards access to joint observables. It need not preserve purity, even though the full state is pure. It is also not a physical collapse caused by an observer forgetting a classical label. It gives the same local statistics whether the inaccessible subsystem is measured, retained in a laboratory, or never inspected.

## 5. A complex example exposes a hidden transpose

The matrix C†C is a useful Gram matrix, but Bob's reduced density in our convention is its transpose. They have identical eigenvalues and opposite off-diagonal imaginary signs. Real examples can conceal this distinction.

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad N=2,\qquad
\rho_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\rho_B=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\i&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
\frac{C^\dagger C}{N}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is the product |0⟩A⊗(|0⟩+i|1⟩)B/√2. Bob's Pauli Y expectation is +1. Reading the untransposed Gram matrix as his state would predict −1. A spectrum or purity check alone would miss the mistake because both matrices are pure and have eigenvalues 1,0.

For a useful diagnostic, reconstruct an observable expectation directly from the global vector and independently from the reduced matrix. Include a genuinely complex state and a Y observable. Exact agreement of local laws is a stronger convention check than agreement of eigenvalues alone.

## 6. Schmidt decomposition follows from a singular-value decomposition

Take a singular-value decomposition of C. Its nonzero singular values are sk, and its corresponding left and right singular vectors are uk and vk. Reading matrix entries as tensor coefficients introduces a conjugation on Bob's vector.

$$
C=U\Sigma V^\dagger,\qquad
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^r\frac{s_k}{\sqrt N}|u_k\rangle\otimes|\overline{v_k}\rangle,\qquad
\lambda_k=\frac{s_k^2}{N},\quad \sum_k\lambda_k=1,\quad
\operatorname{spec}_{\ne0}(\rho_A)=\operatorname{spec}_{\ne0}(\rho_B)=\{\lambda_k\}_{k=1}^r.
$$

Indeed, Cij is the sum of sk Uik times the conjugate of Vjk. This proves both the Schmidt expansion and the equality of the nonzero reduced spectra. Alice has dA−r zero eigenvalues and Bob has dB−r. Different local dimensions therefore do not require identical complete spectral lists.

For a repeated positive singular value, the corresponding columns of U and V can both be rotated by the same unitary W. On Bob's physical Schmidt vectors the rotation becomes the conjugate of W, so the sum of paired products stays fixed. Individual basis vectors within that degenerate sector are not unique. Even nondegenerate vectors retain paired phase freedom; zero eigenspaces have further freedom.

The numerical family reports exact multiplicities and certified eigenvalue intervals. It does not return general Schmidt basis vectors. A derivation of their existence and a numerical computation of a particular basis are different deliverables. Near-degenerate eigenvectors can be unstable even when the invariant subspace and total weight are well resolved.

## 7. Rank one is an exact pure-state separability test

A nonzero pure state is a product precisely when C has rank one. If C=uvᵀ this is immediate. Conversely, choose any nonzero entry Cij. When every two-by-two minor vanishes, each entry is forced to equal a product of one entry from its pivot column and one from its pivot row.

$$
C_{ij}C_{kl}-C_{il}C_{kj}=0\ \text{for all }i,k,j,l
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{rank}C=1,\qquad
u_k=C_{kj},\quad v_l=\frac{C_{il}}{C_{ij}},\quad C=uv^T,\quad
N=\|u\|^2\|v\|^2.
$$

The factorization normalizes to (u/‖u‖)⊗(v/‖v‖). If a subsystem has dimension one, the nonzero table automatically has rank one and there are no two-by-two minors to inspect. The zero table is excluded because it cannot represent a normalized state.

For a globally pure state, product structure is also equivalent to either reduced state having rank one, purity one, or zero von Neumann entropy. These equivalences use global purity. They do not make local purity a complete entanglement detector for arbitrary mixed states.

An arbitrarily small nonzero minor still certifies rank greater than one. Numerical closeness to a product state is a separate approximation question that needs a norm, error tolerance and purpose. Exact classification must not silently replace that question by a rounding rule.

## 8. Entropy measures the distribution of Schmidt weight

The reduced eigenvalues form a probability distribution. Their von Neumann entropy is the Shannon entropy of that distribution; the bases themselves do not enter. We use natural logarithms for nats and divide by ln2 for bits, with 0ln0 defined by its limit as zero.

$$
S_A=S_B=-\sum_{k=1}^r\lambda_k\ln\lambda_k,\qquad
S_{\rm bits}=\frac{S_A}{\ln2},\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^2)=\sum_k\lambda_k^2,\qquad
0\le S_A\le\ln r\le\ln\min(d_A,d_B).
$$

The upper bound follows from the nonnegativity of the relative entropy of λ to the uniform distribution on its r nonzero entries. Equality requires equal weights. A rank-four state need not be maximally entangled: rank counts nonzero weights, while entropy measures their balance. Purity emphasizes large weights and also fails to determine the whole spectrum.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| diag(1,1) | 1/2,1/2 | 1/2 | 1 |
| diag(1,2,3) | 1/14,4/14,9/14 | 1/2 | About 1.198117 |
| diag(1,2,3,4) | 1/30,4/30,9/30,16/30 | 59/150 | About 1.555913 |

The first two examples have the same reduced purity but different Schmidt rank and entropy. The qutrit and ququart examples require their actual local dimensions; replacing them by two-level pictures would remove physical weights. Their entropies in nats are approximately 0.8304717124 and 1.0784767751, respectively.

## 9. Conditional states explain steering without signalling

Choose local rank-one projectors P and Q from nonzero probe vectors. Each apparatus has outcomes given by the projector and its orthogonal complement. For dimensions above two, that complement can have rank greater than one. Joint probabilities come from applying the two local projectors to the global vector.

$$
p_{ab}=\langle\psi|P_a\otimes Q_b|\psi\rangle,\qquad
\widetilde\rho_{B|a}=\operatorname{Tr}_A[(P_a\otimes I)\rho(P_a\otimes I)],\qquad
\rho_{B|a}=\frac{\widetilde\rho_{B|a}}{p_a}\ (p_a>0),\qquad
\sum_a\widetilde\rho_{B|a}=\rho_B.
$$

For the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, Alice's computational-basis outcome 0 selects Bob's state |0⟩ with probability 1/2, and outcome 1 selects |1⟩. Without Alice's label, Bob sees their equally weighted mixture I/2. Changing Alice's measurement changes the labelled decomposition but not this unlabelled state.

A zero-probability event has no normalized conditional state; the result is null rather than a fabricated default vector. For a higher-dimensional complement, the unnormalized conditional state can sum several independent contributions and become mixed even when the global state is pure. Conditioning on that coarse outcome must not be mistaken for conditioning on one resolved basis label.

The last identity is the no-signalling statement for the nonselective local measurement. Communicating Alice's classical outcome can reveal conditional correlations. Merely choosing her apparatus cannot change the probability law available to Bob alone.

## 10. Separate local basis changes from physical dephasing

Local unitaries change C to UACUBᵀ, rotate the reduced matrices by conjugation, and preserve their spectra. A convenient finite test is the Householder reflection H=I−2P: because P²=P=P†, H†H=I. Comparing the full tensor action with the transformed table checks both indexing and spectral invariance.

$$
C'=U_A C U_B^T,\qquad
\rho_A'=U_A\rho_AU_A^\dagger,\quad \rho_B'=U_B\rho_BU_B^\dagger,\qquad
\rho_{\rm diag}=\sum_{ij}\frac{|C_{ij}|^2}{N}|ij\rangle\langle ij|.
$$

The final expression describes dephasing in a specified product basis. It is a mixture of product basis states and is therefore separable. It retains the computational-basis joint probability table but removes off-diagonal coherence. In general it changes local density matrices as well, removing their off-diagonal entries. It preserves both local matrices only when those marginals are already diagonal in the selected bases.

For a Bell state, dephasing keeps the same local I/2 states and the same perfectly correlated computational-basis outcomes, but destroys entanglement. One measurement table and two marginals cannot reconstruct all joint coherence. A drawing that keeps only table populations should therefore not label itself a complete visualization of the quantum state.

## 11. Pure-state partial transpose has a useful analytic spectrum

Use Schmidt coordinates to transpose Bob's matrix indices. A diagonal product |kk⟩ contributes eigenvalue λk. For every pair k<l, the states |kl⟩ and |lk⟩ span a two-by-two block with zero diagonal and off-diagonal magnitude √(λkλl). Its symmetric and antisymmetric combinations give opposite eigenvalues.

$$
\operatorname{spec}(\rho^{T_B})=
\{\lambda_k\}_{k=1}^r\ \cup\ \{+\sqrt{\lambda_k\lambda_l},-\sqrt{\lambda_k\lambda_l}:k<l\}
\ \cup\ \{0\}^{d_A d_B-r^2},\qquad
\mathcal N=\sum_{k<l}\sqrt{\lambda_k\lambda_l},\quad
E_{\mathcal N}=\log_2(1+2\mathcal N).
$$

Our negativity convention is (‖ρᵀᴮ‖₁−1)/2. The Bell value is 1/2. A pure state has a negative partial-transpose eigenvalue exactly when its Schmidt rank exceeds one, in any finite bipartite dimensions. This pure-state result does not establish sufficiency of the PPT criterion for general high-dimensional mixed states.

If some Schmidt weights repeat, values in the displayed list can coincide. The list counts spectral terms with multiplicity, not separate unique eigenspaces. At rank r there are r² nonzero terms, including negative terms, and the remaining dAdB−r² eigenvalues vanish. Summing the negative magnitudes directly avoids subtracting nearly equal trace-norm quantities for weak entanglement.

## 12. Connect the mathematics to AI and larger systems

The same SVD organizes matrix approximation. Keeping the largest R singular values minimizes Frobenius approximation error among rank-R matrices. For the normalized quantum vector, the discarded squared norm is the sum of discarded Schmidt weights. If the retained mass is m>0, normalizing the truncated vector gives squared overlap m with the original state. This is a statement about vector fidelity, not automatically a bound for every downstream learning metric.

Repeated bipartitions lead to tensor-network factorizations. A small bond dimension can represent states with limited Schmidt rank across the corresponding cut; it cannot represent an arbitrary highly entangled state at the same cost. A dataset tensor is not automatically a quantum state, and choosing a tensorization changes the bipartition and therefore the ranks being discussed.

Every finite mixed state also has a purification: diagonalize it and attach an orthonormal environment label to each eigenvector with amplitude equal to the square root of its weight. Tracing the environment returns the original density. Purifications are not unique, and an inaccessible environment does not provide a known classical preparation label.

Three or more parties require additional structure. For example, the three-qubit GHZ state and the W state have different single-party spectra and different two-party correlations after discarding a party. There is generally no single Schmidt expansion that simultaneously diagonalizes all multipartite structure. LOCC conversion, asymptotic resource rates, bound entanglement and experimentally certified witnesses require further study. The two finite calculators do not mark those research questions complete.

## Research extension: closest products, witness margins and heralded filtering

### The largest Schmidt weight has a geometric meaning

The rank test answers whether a product description is exact. To ask how close one product can come, specify a metric. Normalize the coefficient table and use the operator norm from ordinary Euclidean vector norms:

$$
D=C/\sqrt N,\quad \lambda_{\max}=\|D\|_{\rm op}^2,\qquad
 \max_{\|\alpha\|=\|\beta\|=1}|\langle\alpha\otimes\beta|\psi\rangle|^2
 =\max_{\alpha,\beta}|\alpha^\dagger D\overline\beta|^2=\lambda_{\max}.
$$

For fixed Bob vector, Cauchy–Schwarz optimizes Alice's vector. The remaining maximum is the largest singular value of D. A Schmidt pair attains it. Bob's complex conjugation follows our A-major coefficient convention; dropping it can select the wrong physical vector even when the optimum value is unchanged.

Convexity extends the overlap bound from pure products to every separable mixed state. A known reference state therefore defines a Hermitian entanglement witness:

$$
W_\psi=\lambda_{\max}I-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\rm sep}W_\psi)\ge0,\qquad
 \langle\psi|\rho|\psi\rangle>\lambda_{\max}\ \Longrightarrow\ \rho\text{ is entangled}.
$$

Here ψ is the fixed reference, while the tested density ρ may be mixed. A negative witness expectation certifies entanglement; a nonnegative one is inconclusive. The reference, subsystem split and measurement calibration must be specified. This is a geometric certificate, not a claim that a single local histogram measures global fidelity.

For pure product approximants, two useful distances follow from the overlap:

$$
D_{\rm tr}(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1,\qquad
 \min_{\alpha,\beta}D_{\rm tr}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,|\alpha\beta\rangle\langle\alpha\beta|)
 =\sqrt{1-\lambda_{\max}},\qquad
 \min_{\alpha,\beta,\phi}\|\psi-e^{i\phi}\alpha\otimes\beta\|^2=2-2\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}.
$$

The first identity uses the two nonzero eigenvalues of the difference of two pure projectors. The second aligns their global phase before expanding the squared vector norm. Both minima above run over normalized pure product vectors. Neither is asserted to be the closest distance to the entire convex set of separable mixed densities. That larger set can be closer.

### A successful branch can be more entangled than its source

Consider a known two-qubit state already in its Schmidt basis:

$$
|\psi_q\rangle=\sqrt{1-q}|00\rangle+\sqrt q|11\rangle,\quad0\le q\le\tfrac12,\quad
 \rho_B=\operatorname{diag}(1-q,q),\quad\lambda_{\max}=1-q,\quad
 h(q)=-(1-q)\ln(1-q)-q\ln q.
$$

Use 0 ln 0=0 by continuity. Alice can attenuate the larger Schmidt amplitude without touching Bob. The following two Kraus operators include both the retained branch s and the failure flag f:

$$
K_s=\sqrt{\frac q{1-q}}|0\rangle\langle0|+|1\rangle\langle1|,\quad
 K_f=\sqrt{\frac{1-2q}{1-q}}|0\rangle\langle0|,\quad
 K_s^\dagger K_s+K_f^\dagger K_f=I,\qquad
 (K_s\otimes I)\psi_q=\sqrt q(|00\rangle+|11\rangle),\quad p_s=2q,\quad
 (K_f\otimes I)\psi_q=\sqrt{1-2q}|00\rangle,\quad p_f=1-2q.
$$

Squaring the diagonal entries proves completeness. The success vector has equal coefficients, so it is Φ+ after normalization whenever q>0. Failure gives a product whenever q<1/2. At q=0 the success probability is zero and its normalized conditional state is undefined. At q=1/2 failure is the zero-probability branch. Neither endpoint needs division by a fabricated small number.

Why this attenuation? To obtain equal surviving coefficients with a diagonal success operator diag(a,b), one needs a√(1−q)=b√q. The contraction constraint |b|≤1 implies success probability at most 2q within this diagonal-filter class, and the displayed operator attains it. This calculation does not prove a general optimal LOCC conversion theorem. It illustrates the one-pair concentration idea of [Bennett and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9511030), using the known Schmidt weights and basis.

The subnormalized Bob states retain the branch probabilities:

$$
\widetilde\rho_{B|s}=qI_2,\quad
 \widetilde\rho_{B|f}=(1-2q)|0\rangle\langle0|,\qquad
 \widetilde\rho_{B|s}+\widetilde\rho_{B|f}=\rho_B.
$$

Their sum is exactly his original reduced state. A communicated flag identifies a Bell subensemble; Alice's unannounced action cannot change Bob's local probability law. Replacing the sum by only the normalized success branch would silently change the experiment.

### Count every input pair when reporting a resource yield

The successful pair has entropy ln 2, but the branch-weighted remaining entanglement is:

$$
\overline E=p_sE(\Phi^+)+p_fE(00)=2q\ln2\le h(q),\qquad
 h''(q)=-\frac1{q(1-q)}<0\quad(0<q<\tfrac12).
$$

Concavity places h above the chord joining q=0 and q=1/2. This proves the displayed inequality for this filter, with strict inequality in the interior. Concentration improves selected pairs while consuming unsuccessful attempts. The average here is the average pure-branch entropy with the classical flag retained; it is not an instruction to call the entropy of a mixed output an entanglement measure.

Rational amplitudes make exact branch accounting easy:

$$
\psi_u=\frac{|00\rangle+u|11\rangle}{\sqrt{1+u^2}},\quad
 q=\frac{u^2}{1+u^2},\quad\lambda_{\max}=\frac1{1+u^2},\quad
 p_s=\frac{2u^2}{1+u^2},\quad p_f=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\quad0\le u\le1.
$$


```python
from fractions import Fraction as Q
for u in [Q(0), Q(1, 3), Q(1, 2), Q(1)]:
    q = u*u/(1+u*u)
    largest = 1/(1+u*u)
    success, failure = 2*q, 1-2*q
    assert success + failure == 1
    assert q + largest == 1
    print(u, q, largest, success, failure)
```

#### Research check 1: a nearly product source

For q=1/1000000, the retained state is Bell whenever success occurs, but success probability is only1/500000. The original closest-product trace distance is1/1000. A plot showing only retained Bell pairs would hide the dominant failed attempts. At exactly q=0 there are no retained pairs to normalize.

#### Research check 2: pure products versus separable mixtures

A Bell pair has largest Schmidt weight1/2, so every pure product is at trace distance at least1/√2. Yet the separable mixture of 00 and11 is at distance1/2: subtracting it from the Bell projector leaves an off-diagonal block with eigenvalues ±1/2. Thus the pure-product distance formula cannot be relabelled as distance to all separable states.

#### Research check 3: no flag, no remote change

At q=1/10, the success and failure Bob matrices are I/10 and(4/5)|0⟩⟨0|. Their sum is diag(9/10,1/10), not I/2. Bob sees I/2 only within the success subensemble identified by Alice's message. Entanglement concentration and no-signalling are consistent.

## 13. Exercises with explanations

**1. Count coordinates before flattening.** A has dimension two and B has dimension three. Where does C12 occur in the A-major vector, and how many coordinates would a direct sum have?

**Explanation.** The zero-based index is 1×3+2=5, the sixth coordinate of the six-dimensional tensor product. The direct sum would have five coordinates and would model different sectors. Index 5 therefore cannot be carried unchanged into the swapped ordering without specifying the new dimensions.

**2. Detect a complex convention error.** Use the C of section 5 to compute Bob's Y mean. Would C†C/N give the same answer?

**Explanation.** Bob is in (1,i)/√2, a +1 eigenvector of Y, so his mean is +1. The untransposed Gram matrix gives −1. Its eigenvalues and purity still agree with Bob's true state; checking only those invariants cannot catch the mistaken transpose.

**3. Reconstruct product factors.** Let C have rows (1,i,2) and (2,2i,4). Find normalized local factors and verify the norm.

**Explanation.** Use the pivot C00=1. The factors are u=(1,2) and v=(1,i,2), with squared norms 5 and 6. Their outer product uvᵀ reproduces every entry and N=30. Normalizing gives u/√5 and v/√6. Every two-by-two minor vanishes and both reduced states are pure despite the six-dimensional joint space.

**4. Same purity, different entanglement spectrum.** Compare diag(1,1) and diag(1,2,3) from section 8. Can a purity of 1/2 identify a Bell pair?

**Explanation.** No. The first has weights 1/2,1/2 and one bit of entropy. The second has three nonzero weights 1/14,4/14,9/14, also with purity 1/2, but about 1.198117 bits. The local dimensions, rank and full spectrum matter. Neither example is a mixed global state.

**5. A coarse complement can leave a mixed state.** Prepare (|00⟩+|11⟩+|22⟩)/√3 and let Alice test P=|0⟩⟨0| versus I−P. Describe Bob's conditional and nonselective states.

**Explanation.** Outcome P has probability 1/3 and leaves |0⟩. The complement has probability 2/3 and leaves (|1⟩⟨1|+|2⟩⟨2|)/2, with purity 1/2. Weighting these conditionals gives I3/3. Resolving labels 1 and 2 would be a different, finer measurement.

**6. Read partial-transpose multiplicities.** For the same maximally entangled qutrit state, give the negativity and the number of positive and negative eigenvalues.

**Explanation.** Three diagonal terms are +1/3. The three unordered pairs add another three +1/3 terms and three −1/3 terms. Thus six eigenvalues are positive, three are negative and none vanish in dimension nine. The negativity is 1 and log-negativity is log₂3. Identical values do not represent nine distinct eigenspaces.

## 14. Use the finite model with its mathematical contract

The pure family accepts local dimensions 1 through 4, all sixteen dimension pairs, and a nonzero raw complex coefficient table. Its 50 owned integer fields also include two nonzero local probe vectors; each integer has magnitude at most 10⁶. Inactive state and probe coordinates must be zero. A probe specifies a rank-one projector, with its full orthogonal complement as the second outcome.

Density entries, partial traces, rank, minors, characteristic coefficients, conditional matrices and local reflections are exact complex rationals. General reduced spectra are isolated by exact square-free factorization and Sturm counts at 1024 bits. Nonlinear quantities use 768-bit working intervals and outward 512-bit observations. Known rational point roots and unresolved rationality are distinguished; a non-point isolating interval is not proof that its root is irrational. A null numerical projection means the display cannot safely provide a machine number, while its rational enclosure can remain informative.
