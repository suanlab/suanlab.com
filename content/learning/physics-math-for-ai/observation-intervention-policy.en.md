## Three questions about the same system

How well do cases receiving X=1 perform? What if everyone received X=1? What if an external policy assigned X=1 with probability r? These questions use different populations or assignment mechanisms. A conditional prediction, fixed intervention and stochastic policy should therefore have distinct notation and readouts.

Let Z indicate task difficulty, X indicate a system configuration, and Y indicate a binary outcome. The outcome mean is a transparent teaching mechanism, not a fitted performance law:

$$
q(z,x)=0.1+0.6z+0.2x.
$$

The coefficient of X is a mean effect within each Z stratum. A Bernoulli outcome is generated by comparing an independent uniform noise U_Y with q(z,x). Z has probability pi of being one, while observational assignment uses e_z=P(X=1|Z=z).

## Observation reweights the difficulty mix

For a query X=a, use Bayes' rule:

$$
P(Z=z\mid X=a)=\frac{P(X=a\mid Z=z)P(Z=z)}{P(X=a)}.
$$

The observed mean averages q(z,a) over this posterior stratum mix. At pi=0.5, e_0=0.1 and e_1=0.9, observed X=1 cases are 90% difficult. Their mean is 0.1(0.3)+0.9(0.9)=0.84. This predicts an observed group; it does not describe what forcing X would do to the original population. The table displays both P(Z) and P(Z|X=a) to expose the difference.

## Fixed intervention retains the target population

Replace assignment by X=a, retain P(Z) and the outcome mechanism, and integrate:

$$
\mu_a=E[Y\mid do(X=a)]=\sum_z q(z,a)P(Z=z).
$$

With the same parameters, mu_1=0.5(0.3)+0.5(0.9)=0.6 and mu_0=0.4. The average treatment effect is 0.2. The DAG crosses out Z→X because the fixed assignment no longer uses difficulty. Its outgoing X→Y edge remains.

## An external stochastic policy

Draw a new policy coin independently of Z and U_Y, assigning X=1 with probability r. Conditional on that coin, each branch follows a fixed intervention. Hence

$$
\mu(r)=(1-r)\mu_0+r\mu_1=0.1+0.6\pi+0.2r.
$$

The blue curve computes this policy mean; its dot marks the current r. The orange horizontal reference is the observed query mean, independent of r. At r=0.5 and pi=0.5 the policy mean is 0.5. The line is linear because this policy mixes two fixed interventions with fixed weights. A policy depending on Z would require stratum-dependent weights; feedback, interference or changing mechanisms would require another model.

## Definition is different from identification

Set pi=1, e_0=e_1=1 and query a=0. Every observational case has X=1. P(X=0)=0 makes the observed conditional undefined, not zero. The declared SCM still defines mu_0=0.7, because it specifies the outcome mechanism under the unseen assignment.

The lab separately reports whether backdoor adjustment has positive support for the query in each stratum with positive target mass. It displays the identified mean only when that requirement holds. A model-defined oracle does not supply empirical evidence for its unobserved assumptions. In an observational study, identification additionally needs consistency and conditional exchangeability; here the independent-noise construction explicitly supplies them.

## A stochastic intervention can respect existing support

An external coin uses one action probability for everyone. A different intervention can use a measured pre-treatment Z. For a finite odds multiplier delta>0, transform the natural propensity e(z) into

$$
g_\delta(1\mid z)=\frac{\delta e(z)}{1-e(z)+\delta e(z)},\quad
V(\delta)=\sum_zP(z)\sum_xg_\delta(x\mid z)q(z,x).
$$

At delta=1 it retains the natural assignment probabilities. At e=0 or e=1 the same action remains deterministic: it never demands an action structurally absent in that stratum. For positive observational action probability, the importance ratio is bounded by max(delta,1/delta). These are support and weight properties of this policy family, not exchangeability proofs.

Holding the propensity and outcome mechanisms fixed, differentiation gives dV/dlog(delta)=sum_z P(z)*g_delta*(1−g_delta)*(q(z,1)−q(z,0)). Strata with deterministic logging contribute zero. The example uses the current outcome law and pi=1/2,e=(1/10,9/10), but this Z-dependent odds intervention is auxiliary and does not change the independent-coin control.

| Odds multiplier | Policy outcome mean |
|---|---:|
| 1/2 | 509/1045 |
| 1 | 1/2 |
| 2 | 536/1045 |

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F
e=[F(1,10),F(9,10)];P=[F(1,2)]*2
q=lambda z,x:F(1,10)+F(3,5)*z+F(1,5)*x
for delta in (F(1,2),F(1),F(2)):
    g=[delta*p/(1-p+delta*p) for p in e]
    value=sum(P[z]*((1-g[z])*q(z,0)+g[z]*q(z,1)) for z in range(2))
    derivative=sum(P[z]*g[z]*(1-g[z])*(q(z,1)-q(z,0)) for z in range(2))
    for p in (F(0),*e,F(1)):
        treated=delta*p/(1-p+delta*p)
        for observed,target in ((p,treated),(1-p,1-treated)):
            assert (target==0 if observed==0 else target/observed<=max(delta,1/delta))
    print(delta,value,derivative)
```

A policy defined through the population propensity is itself a distribution-dependent estimand. Estimating that propensity and evaluating the resulting policy requires an honest procedure and uncertainty analysis. Interference, feedback or changed outcome mechanisms still require a new model. Respecting overlap reduces extrapolation; it does not make hidden confounding disappear.

The observed query also uses log normalization: `logQueryMass` can be finite when its raw `queryMass` underflows to zero. This preserves the conditional observation while backdoor adjustment still checks every target-positive stratum; the two support questions are distinct.

For broader identification and estimation results, see [Kennedy’s incremental intervention analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00211). The finite one-step derivation above is not its full longitudinal efficiency theory.

## Worked exercise and solution

Set pi=0.5 and e_0=e_1=0.5. Compare the observed X=1 mean, fixed do(1) mean and policy mean at r=0.5.

**Solution.** Assignment is independent of Z, so the observed stratum mix equals P(Z). The observed and fixed do(1) means both equal 0.6. The policy mean is 0.5 because half the population follows do(0), whose mean is 0.4. Agreement between observation and one fixed intervention does not make every stochastic policy identical to that intervention.

## Connection to AI policy evaluation

When changing a recommender or controller, specify who is included, how actions are assigned, which environment mechanisms stay invariant, and the outcome horizon. Logged conditional performance may reflect user or task selection. This chapter calculates known population mechanisms; later identification and off-policy chapters address what can be recovered when those mechanisms are unknown.
