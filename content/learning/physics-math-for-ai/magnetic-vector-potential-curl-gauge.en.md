## Different maps can describe the same magnetic field

In electrostatics, a scalar potential produces a field by a gradient. A magnetic vector potential produces its field by a curl. This extra representation is useful, but it is not unique: a carefully chosen change in the potential can leave the physical field unchanged.

The goal is not to memorize a transformation. We will construct a vector potential for the current cylinder from the preceding lesson, differentiate it, and compare two visibly different potentials that describe the same magnetic preparation. We will also distinguish a potential's closed integral from Ampere's magnetic circulation.

## Define the representation and its units

We define

$$
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A.
$$

Magnetic field has units T, so $\mathbf A$ has units T·m. It is a three-component field, not another name for $\mathbf B$. A local value of $\mathbf A$ does not by itself determine the field; its spatial variation matters.

For sufficiently smooth potentials, the divergence of a curl is zero. Parameterizing $\mathbf B$ this way therefore enforces a local differential constraint structurally. The converse construction requires attention to domain and global conditions. Representability on a local patch is not automatically the same as a single global representation with specified boundaries.

## Return to a prescribed steady current

Take the infinite cylinder of radius $a$, carrying uniform total current $I$ along positive $z$. The permeability $\mu$ is constant. The earlier lesson gives signed azimuthal field $\mu Ir/(2\pi a^2)$ inside and $\mu I/(2\pi r)$ outside.

This remains a prescribed static source, not a finite open wire, a conductivity model or a mechanical equilibrium calculation. The teaching default $\mu=4\pi\times10^{-7}$ T·m/A is a rounded approximation, not an exact modern SI constant.

Choose an axial vector potential,

$$
\mathbf A_{\mathrm{src}}=(0,0,A_z(r)),\qquad
r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}.
$$

An axial potential can create a transverse magnetic field because curl mixes components and spatial derivatives. The potential vector does not need to point along the magnetic field.

## Construct a continuous potential with a finite reference

Set the source reference to $A_z(a)=0$. A suitable expression is

$$
A_z(r)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\mu I}{4\pi}\left(1-\dfrac{r^2}{a^2}\right),&0\le r\le a,\\
-\dfrac{\mu I}{2\pi}\log(r/a),&r>a.
\end{cases}
$$

The interior derivative is linear and the exterior derivative is proportional to $1/r$. The logarithm is dimensionless because its argument is a ratio of lengths. Writing $\log r$ without declaring a reference length would hide a units problem.

The infinite source does not admit this logarithmic $A_z$ with a zero limit at infinity. A boundary convention appropriate for a localized source cannot simply be copied here. Adding a constant to $A_z$ changes the chosen representation but not the field; this constant can itself be generated by a smooth gauge function proportional to $z$.

## Check the curl sign in Cartesian coordinates

With only an axial component,

$$
\mathbf B=\left(\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial y},
-\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial x},0\right).
$$

Inside the cylinder, let $c=\mu I/(2\pi a^2)$. Direct differentiation gives

$$
\nabla A_z=(-cx,-cy,0),\qquad
\mathbf B=c(-y,x,0).
$$

At positive $x$, positive current produces positive $B_y$, matching the right-hand convention. At the axis the magnetic vector is zero while $A_z(0)=\mu I/(4\pi)$ is finite. There is no division by an undefined azimuthal basis in this Cartesian calculation.

For $r>0$, the same field is summarized by $B_\phi=-dA_z/dr$. Check the minus sign against the Cartesian components before using a cylindrical formula.

## The interface has a different regularity order

At $r=a$, the two expressions for $A_z$ agree and their first radial derivatives agree:

$$
A_z(a^-)=A_z(a^+)=0,\qquad
A_z'(a^-)=A_z'(a^+)=-\frac{\mu I}{2\pi a}.
$$

For nonzero current, second radial derivatives differ:

$$
A_z''(a^-)=-\frac{\mu I}{2\pi a^2},\qquad
A_z''(a^+)=+\frac{\mu I}{2\pi a^2}.
$$

Thus $A_z$ is C¹ but not C² across the interface. Its curl, the magnetic field, is defined and continuous there. The magnetic field's own radial derivative is not defined there. These statements concern different derivative orders and are compatible.

Away from the interface, the source potential has Laplacian $-\mu I/(\pi a^2)$ inside and zero outside. At the axis, compute that result from Cartesian second derivatives; do not evaluate an apparent $A_z'/r$ as $0/0$. At zero current the jump disappears. The implementation preserves both one-sided second derivatives and leaves the nonzero-current classical interface value unavailable.

## A concrete smooth gauge family

Choose a static, smooth, single-valued scalar function

$$
\chi(\mathbf x)=\frac{\alpha}{2}(x^2+y^2+z^2)+gxyz+c_z z,
\qquad \mathbf A'=\mathbf A+\nabla\chi.
$$

Here $\chi$ has units T·m², $\alpha$ has units T, $g$ has units T/m and $c_z$ has units T·m. Its gradient is

$$
\nabla\chi=(\alpha x+gyz,\ \alpha y+gxz,\ \alpha z+gxy+c_z).
$$

The curl of this gradient vanishes because mixed partial derivatives commute. Therefore $\mathbf A'$ gives exactly the same $\mathbf B$. It can have nonzero x and y components even though the original source potential was purely axial.

This is a representation change. Adding a uniform physical magnetic background instead changes $\mathbf B$ and is a different preparation. A uniform background may itself have a vector potential, but it cannot be obtained merely by adding a smooth gradient to the old potential.

## Gauge choice does not disappear from the representation

The source potential is independent of $z$ and has zero divergence. The transformed divergence is

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf A'=\nabla\cdot\mathbf A+\nabla^2\chi=3\alpha.
$$

A nonzero $\alpha$ changes the divergence while leaving the magnetic field unchanged. The condition $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0$ is called Coulomb gauge; it is a choice of representative, not a separately measured magnetic law.

Even that condition does not always fix the representative uniquely. With $\alpha=0$, the $gxyz$ and $c_z z$ terms remain harmonic, so their gradients preserve zero divergence. Boundary and global conditions matter. For AI reconstruction, a penalty on $\mathbf A$ or its divergence selects representations as well as physical fields; make that distinction explicit.

## A worked observation with real units

Use $a=1$ m, $I=1$ A and the teaching permeability. At $(0.5,0,0)$ m,

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Source $A_z$ | 0.075 μT·m |
| Source $B_y$ | 0.1 μT |
| Source $B_x,B_z$ | 0 T |

Set $\alpha=0.2$ μT, $g=0$ and $c_z=0$. Then the gauge gradient at this point is (0.1,0,0) μT·m. The transformed potential is (0.1,0,0.075) μT·m, while the magnetic field is still (0,0.1,0) μT. Its divergence as a potential is now 0.6 μT, which has units T, not the T/m units of magnetic-field divergence.

At $r=2$ m, the source axial potential is approximately −0.1386294 μT·m. The sign of a potential component does not alone give the magnetic field direction; differentiation does.

## A closed potential integral measures magnetic flux

For an oriented surface and its boundary, Stokes's theorem gives

$$
\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell
=\iint_S\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf n\,dS.
$$

Both sides have units T·m², or weber. Ampere's law integrates $\mathbf B$ instead and has units T·m. These are two different line integrals with different physical interpretations.

Choose a rectangle in the xz plane at $y=0$: move from left to right along the bottom, then upward, then left along the top and back down. This order has normal in the negative y direction. For the axial source potential, only the vertical edges contribute:

$$
\oint\mathbf A_{\mathrm{src}}\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell
=(z_{\mathrm{top}}-z_{\mathrm{bottom}})
[A_z(x_{\mathrm{right}})-A_z(x_{\mathrm{left}})].
$$

Take left=0.25 m, right=1.5 m and height=1 m. The axial values are 0.09375 and approximately −0.08109302 μT·m. Circulation is therefore −0.17484302 μWb. The flux is negative because $B_y$ is positive over this rectangle while its oriented normal points toward negative y.

Adding a gauge gradient changes individual edge integrals but its closed sum is zero. Reversing the loop reverses circulation and flux. A zero-width or zero-height rectangle is degenerate and has zero oriented area; it does not define an average normal field by division.

## Why this integral is not mechanical work

The closed integral of $\mathbf A$ has magnetic-flux units. Multiplying it by charge does not give energy; it has action units. A magnetic Lorentz force is perpendicular to velocity and by itself does no instantaneous mechanical work. Do not label a nonzero potential circulation as work done by the magnetic field.

Potentials play an essential role in quantum descriptions, including phases, but that requires additional structure and a later lesson. Gauge redundancy does not mean that potentials can be discarded or changed without respecting the allowed transformation and domain.

## Local zero curl is not every global gauge transformation

On a domain excluding the z axis, a field proportional to $\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}/r$ has zero local curl but can have nonzero circulation around the hole. It is locally a gradient of an angular expression, but that angle is not a globally single-valued smooth scalar around the axis.

Our gauge family explicitly uses single-valued polynomials, so its closed gradient integral vanishes. Do not enlarge the family to arbitrary locally curl-free additions while claiming identical global gauge conditions. Topology, singularities and boundary data are part of the equivalence statement.

## Static restrictions, numerical checks and research use

In SI the general potential representation is $\mathbf E=-\nabla V-\partial_t\mathbf A$. A time-dependent gauge requires the simultaneous change $V'=V-\partial_t\chi$ along with $\mathbf A'=\mathbf A+\nabla\chi$. Our numerical family is static. A Gaussian-unit lecture's extra $1/c$ coefficient must not be copied into this SI expression.

The implementation uses a regular interior expression and a `log1p` exterior expression referenced to $a$. Potential, gradient, magnetic field and second-derivative availability are separate. Lost nonzero intermediates are arithmetic limitations, not zeros. The closed rectangle residual shares endpoint formulas and is only a consistency diagnostic.

Independent references instead differentiate the actual gauged potential numerically and integrate the magnetic field over rectangle surfaces, splitting at source interfaces. This separates algebraic identities from independent evidence. An AI model of $\mathbf A$ should be checked for curl accuracy, interface regularity, reference/gauge behavior and oriented flux, not merely for agreement with one arbitrary representative.

## Research extension: periodic potentials cannot carry a mean magnetic flux

### Specify periodicity as part of the representation

The infinite-cylinder gauge example above lives in its declared nonperiodic source geometry. Change to a separate static example: a cubic cell of side L>0 with opposite faces identified. Assume a globally single-valued, smooth periodic vector potential, at least C² for the differential identities used here. Its units are T m. Periodic refers to the potential itself, not just to its derivatives.

Integrate each component of its curl over the cell. Opposite-face derivative contributions cancel, so the magnetic field produced by that representation has zero cell mean. This is a constraint of the representation and boundary conditions.

$$
\mathcal D=[0,L)^3,\quad\mathbf A(\mathbf x+L\mathbf e_i)=\mathbf A(\mathbf x),\quad\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A:\qquad\langle\mathbf B\rangle=\frac1{L^3}\int_{\mathcal D}\nabla\times\mathbf A\,d^3x=0.
$$

$$
\Phi_z(z)=\int_0^L\!\int_0^LB_z(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy=\oint_{\partial[0,L]^2}\mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=0\qquad\text{for a smooth periodic }\mathbf A.
$$

### Distinguish one fundamental face from a closed surface

The same obstruction follows from Stokes on a square cell face. Its four edge integrals cancel in opposite pairs when A is periodic. Cyclic permutation gives the result for the other two face orientations.

A uniform nonzero field is divergence-free and satisfies zero flux through the complete closed cube. Yet its flux through an individual fundamental xy face is not zero. Thus it is a valid prescribed magnetic field but cannot be curl of one smooth globally periodic A. Nonzero face flux is not a magnetic monopole.

$$
\mathbf B_{\mathrm{const}}=(0,0,B_0),\quad B_0\ne0:\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B_{\mathrm{const}}=0,\quad\Phi_z^+=B_0L^2,\quad\Phi_z^-=-B_0L^2,\quad\int_{\partial\mathcal D}\mathbf B_{\mathrm{const}}\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=0.
$$

### Invert only the nonzero Fourier modes

For a zero-mean divergence-free finite Fourier sum, write modes as exp(i k dot x). The coefficient below solves curl A=B and Coulomb gauge mode by mode. The vector triple-product identity proves both statements. Pair conjugate coefficients at k and −k when constructing real fields. Extending this construction to infinite sums requires suitable regularity and convergence assumptions.

Wave number has units 1/m, so the coefficient for A has units T m. The formula does not apply at k=0. Replacing its zero denominator by a small numerical constant cannot create the missing mean magnetic field.

$$
\mathbf k=\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbf n\ne0,\quad\mathbf k\cdot\widehat{\mathbf B}_{\mathbf k}=0:\quad\widehat{\mathbf A}_{\mathbf k}=\frac{i\mathbf k\times\widehat{\mathbf B}_{\mathbf k}}{|\mathbf k|^2},\quad i\mathbf k\times\widehat{\mathbf A}_{\mathbf k}=\widehat{\mathbf B}_{\mathbf k},\quad i\mathbf k\cdot\widehat{\mathbf A}_{\mathbf k}=0.
$$

$$
\langle\nabla\chi\rangle=0\quad(\chi\text{ smooth and periodic}),\qquad\nabla\times\mathbf a_0=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf a_0=0,\quad\oint_{C_i}\mathbf a_0\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell=(a_0)_iL.
$$

### Keep the remaining constant potential separate

A periodic scalar gauge changes a nonzero coefficient by i k times its scalar coefficient; this does not change curl. Coulomb gauge fixes that nonzero-mode gradient freedom. A constant potential vector a0 has zero curl and divergence but is still not a gradient of a smooth periodic scalar unless it is zero: integrate around the corresponding fundamental cycle.

Choosing the mean of the periodic potential is an extra representation convention, not something inferred from B or div A=0. On ordinary space a0 is the gradient of a0 dot x, but that scalar is not periodic. Keep the allowed domain and gauge class fixed. This classical supplement makes no quantum-observability claim about constant potentials.

### Represent a mean plus a finite fluctuating mode

Use a field with a uniform z component and one cosine fluctuation. The sinusoidal potential is periodic and represents the fluctuation. The affine background potential supplies the mean. Both are divergence-free as potentials in this example, but their sum is not periodic when B0 is nonzero. Curl uses both dA_y/dx and −dA_x/dy; each affine component contributes B0/2. A plot of A_y on one slice does not include the other derivative.

$$
k=\frac{2\pi}{L},\quad\mathbf B_{\mathrm{tar}}=(0,0,B_0+B_1\cos kx):\qquad\mathbf A_{\mathrm p}=\left(0,\frac{B_1}{k}\sin kx,0\right),\quad\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}}=\frac{B_0}{2}(-y,x,0),\quad\nabla\times(\mathbf A_{\mathrm p}+\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}})=\mathbf B_{\mathrm{tar}}.
$$

$$
\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}}(\mathbf x+L\mathbf e_x)-\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}}(\mathbf x)=\left(0,\frac{B_0L}{2},0\right),\qquad\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}}(\mathbf x+L\mathbf e_y)-\mathbf A_{\mathrm{bg}}(\mathbf x)=\left(-\frac{B_0L}{2},0,0\right).
$$

### Quantify what a periodic-only learner cannot fit

The affine jumps below are required by this representation. They are not discontinuities in the magnetic field, which stays smooth and periodic. A method can instead keep the constant magnetic background as an explicit output and represent only the zero-mean part by a periodic curl; a corresponding global potential still needs appropriate nonperiodic or patch data.

For any smooth periodic trial potential, split the field error into its constant and zero-mean parts. Their cell-average cross term vanishes. This gives an irreducible squared error even with perfect optimization and arbitrarily rich periodic potential models. The sinusoidal potential above attains the bound for this field family. This is a cell-integral error floor; a loss evaluated at finitely many points can miss it through undersampling.

$$
\mathbf B_{\mathrm{fluc}}=(0,0,B_1\cos kx):\quad\left\langle\left|\nabla\times\widetilde{\mathbf A}_{\mathrm p}-\mathbf B_{\mathrm{tar}}\right|^2\right\rangle=B_0^2+\left\langle\left|\nabla\times\widetilde{\mathbf A}_{\mathrm p}-\mathbf B_{\mathrm{fluc}}\right|^2\right\rangle\ge B_0^2\quad(\widetilde{\mathbf A}_{\mathrm p}\text{ smooth and periodic}).
$$

### Work an example with flux and potential units

Take L=2 m, B_ref=1 microtesla and B1=B_ref. For B0=0.5 B_ref, flux through the positive-z face is 2 microweber; the opposite outward face has −2 microweber. The minimum cell RMS error of a periodic-only curl is 0.5 microtesla, while the affine A_y jump across x is 0.5 microtesla m.

The table normalizes flux by B_ref L², RMS by B_ref and the A_y jump by B_ref L. It is an exact calculation for this static family, not a magnetohydrodynamic evolution or a convergence test of a particular solver.

| B0 / B_ref | face flux ratio | minimum RMS ratio | A_y jump ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -1 | -1 | 1 | -0.5 |
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.25 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0.5 |

```python
# Normalize B by B_ref and lengths by L where stated.
for mean_ratio in [-1, 0, .5, 1]:
    face_flux_ratio = mean_ratio
    minimum_rms_ratio = abs(mean_ratio)
    potential_jump_ratio = mean_ratio/2
    print(*(format(t, '.9g') for t in
            [mean_ratio, face_flux_ratio,
             minimum_rms_ratio, potential_jump_ratio]))
```

In the native zero-current cylinder, a constant axial gauge gradient changes A but leaves B zero. Its scalar gauge c_z z is valid on ordinary space and is not periodic in z when c_z is nonzero. This checks the domain distinction, not a new periodic-cell feature in the cylinder lab.

[Mocz and colleagues, section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.02310#S2.SS3), discuss separating periodic and mean-field contributions. The face cancellation, Fourier inverse, remaining constant-potential freedom and error floor above are derived directly for this smooth static cell. No claim about that paper’s numerical MHD accuracy is used as evidence for our supplement.

### Research questions with explanations

**Does nonzero flux through one cell face violate div B=0?** **Answer:** No. Opposite outward faces cancel. The obstruction is the assumed globally periodic potential, not a magnetic charge.

**Can a small denominator at the zero Fourier mode restore the mean field?** **Answer:** No. Curl annihilates the zero potential mode. Carry the mean magnetic field separately or change the representation.

**Does Coulomb gauge fix every component of periodic A?** **Answer:** No. The constant vector remains unconstrained by curl and divergence; its relation to a scalar gauge depends on the allowed periodicity class.

## Exercises with explanations

### Exercise 1 — A nonzero axis potential

Must the magnetic field be nonzero if $A_z(0)$ is nonzero?

**Answer.** No. The interior gradient vanishes on the axis, so curl is zero there even though $A_z(0)=\mu I/(4\pi)$.

### Exercise 2 — Add a quadratic gauge

Does nonzero $\alpha$ change the worked magnetic observation?

**Answer.** It changes the potential components and divergence by $3\alpha$, but leaves the magnetic field unchanged. It may leave the chosen Coulomb gauge.

### Exercise 3 — Different derivative orders

Is curl A unavailable at the current interface because the second derivative of $A_z$ fails there?

**Answer.** No. Curl uses first derivatives, which agree across the interface. The second derivatives and the magnetic field's first derivatives are the quantities lacking common classical values.

### Exercise 4 — Reverse the rectangle

Reverse the worked loop orientation. What happens?

**Answer.** Potential circulation and magnetic flux both become +0.17484302 μWb. The physical field has not reversed; the measurement orientation has.

### Exercise 5 — A units check

Can $\oint A\cdot dl$ be substituted into Ampere's law as magnetic circulation?

**Answer.** No. It has units T·m² and equals magnetic flux. Ampere's integral of B has units T·m. Neither integral alone is magnetic mechanical work.

### Exercise 6 — A residual gauge freedom

With $\alpha=0$, can a nonzero cubic gauge preserve zero divergence of A?

**Answer.** Yes, because the polynomial $gxyz$ is harmonic. A divergence condition alone need not fix the representative; examine boundaries, topology and the allowed gauge class.
