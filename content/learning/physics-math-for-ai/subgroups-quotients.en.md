## Substructures must respect operations

A subgroup is a subset that forms a group under the original operation. It includes the identity and inverses. In an additive residue group, the subgroup generated by h contains every repeated sum of h. At n=6,h=2, the sequence reaches 0,2,4 and then repeats. The plot displays coset labels, not physical magnitudes.

$$
H=\langle h\rangle=\{kh\bmod n:k\in\mathbb Z\},\quad |H|=\frac n{\gcd(n,h)}.
$$

## What a coset identifies

The coset x+H contains x plus every subgroup element. Two cosets agree exactly when their representatives differ by an element of H. In the default example, the even residues form H and the odd residues form 1+H. These disjoint sets cover the original group. Check the table to see that choosing another representative within a coset produces the same set.

$$
x\sim y\iff x-y\in H,\qquad (x+H)+(y+H)=(x+y)+H.
$$

## Quotient operations and normality

The quotient adds cosets rather than original elements. Its result must be independent of representative choices. Our group is abelian, so every subgroup is normal and the quotient operation works. In a general noncommutative group, an arbitrary subgroup does not define a quotient group. For rings, quotient rings require ideals; a subring alone is insufficient.

$$
|G/H|=\gcd(n,h),\qquad |G|=|H|\,|G/H|.
$$

## Extreme cases and structure

If h=0, then H={0} and the quotient retains every original distinction. If h=1, H is the whole group and only one quotient element remains. The default quotient is isomorphic to Z2, and the natural parity map has kernel H. This clarifies which distinctions a representation removes. In AI, identifying symmetry-related inputs is useful only when the task permits their distinctions to be discarded.

## Exercises with explanations

1. What is the size of ⟨2⟩ in Z8? **Four; the quotient has two elements.**
2. Can two cosets overlap partially? **No: they are equal or disjoint.**
3. Must subgroup and quotient sizes match? **No: their product is the original size.**
