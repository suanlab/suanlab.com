## Go beyond the two-dimensional area test

The earlier oscillator-integrator lesson compared discrete update rules in a two-dimensional phase plane. There, determinant one is sufficient for a linear map to preserve the canonical symplectic form. Two canonical pairs require a stronger test. A transformation can preserve four-dimensional volume while changing the pairing between position and momentum variations.

Our experiment studies explicit linear maps on a four-dimensional phase space. It does not introduce another oscillator integrator. The moving query point traces a constructed boundary used to measure action; it is not a solution of a physical time-evolution problem.

## Define the form before drawing a projection

Use the order z=(q1,q2,p1,p2), with positions in meters and momenta in kg m/s. The canonical two-form is

$$
\omega=dq_1\wedge dp_1+dq_2\wedge dp_2.
$$

A wedge product evaluates two tangent vectors by an oriented determinant. For variations u and v,

$$
\omega(u,v)=u_{q_1}v_{p_1}-u_{p_1}v_{q_1}
+u_{q_2}v_{p_2}-u_{p_2}v_{q_2}.
$$

Each contribution has action units Js. The result can be negative or zero for nonzero vectors. It is not a length, a positive area magnitude, or an inner product. In particular omega(u,u)=0 does not mean that u is a zero tangent vector.

A symplectic manifold is a smooth even-dimensional manifold equipped with a closed, nondegenerate two-form. Closed means its exterior derivative is zero. Nondegenerate means that a vector pairing to zero with every vector must itself be zero. On this Cartesian space the coefficients are constant, so closedness holds directly, while the canonical matrix is invertible. These facts differ from a coordinate chart failing at the origin; this chart is regular there.

## Pull a two-form back through a map

For a linear coordinate map z'=Mz, variations become Mu and Mv. Preservation requires

$$
\omega(Mu,Mv)=\omega(u,v)\quad\text{for every }u,v.
$$

In the chosen order, write

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M^TJM=J.
$$

Checking this matrix identity checks all basis-vector pairings and hence all tangent pairs by bilinearity. Checking one selected parallelogram is weaker. The laboratory reports the full exact matrix defect separately from the selected probe's area change.

The previous Poisson-bracket convention gives an equivalent canonical-coordinate test MJM^T=J for invertible M. Keep the argument order and sign convention consistent. Do not identify a matrix of form coefficients, a Poisson coefficient matrix and a metric just because they have the same size.

## Track the units of matrix blocks

A phase map mixes coordinate kinds. Its q-from-q and p-from-p blocks are dimensionless. Its q-from-p block has units s/kg, while its p-from-q block has units kg/s. The numerical matrices use fixed SI reference units so their products remain meaningful.

The coefficients of the pulled-back form have units kg/s in a q-q block, dimensionless in a q-p block, and s/kg in a p-p block. Poisson coefficient blocks have the opposite q-q versus p-p units. A single unweighted Frobenius norm across all these blocks would mix units. Our structural claim is the exact vanishing of every coefficient with its own unit, not a small dimensionally ambiguous norm.

## First use reciprocal scaling

For positive dimensionless a and b,

$$
D=\operatorname{diag}(a,b,1/a,1/b).
$$

The first pair's oriented action area gains a factor a times 1/a, and the second gains b times 1/b. Both remain unchanged. Thus D is symplectic and has determinant one.

For a=2 and b=1, a probe with u in the q1 direction and v in the p1 direction has original area 1 Js and transformed area 1 Js. Its coordinate width doubles while its momentum height halves. The graph's appearance can change while the structural quantity stays fixed. An orthogonal or length-preserving transformation is not required.

## Add coupled shears from generating actions

Let h and c be dimensionless amplitudes. Use reference coefficient units kg/s for

$$
B=\begin{pmatrix}h&c\\c&-h\end{pmatrix}
$$

and s/kg for

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}h&c\\c&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The momentum shear K sends (q,p) to (q,p+Bq). The position shear T sends it to (q+Cp,p). Symmetry of B and C makes each map symplectic: the extra q-q or p-p wedge terms cancel. A nonsymmetric shear would not generally have this property.

These are finite canonical transformations generated by quadratic actions. K is the unit-parameter Hamiltonian flow of −q^TBq/2; T uses p^TCp/2. Their parameter is dimensionless, so these action-valued generators need not be physical energies. The code applies the exact finite maps rather than numerically integrating them.

The selectable families are D, KD, TKD and a volume-preserving comparison family defined below. Matrix multiplication order matters: TKD scales first, then changes momentum, then changes position.

## Preserve the total form while exchanging projected areas

Take a=b=1, h=0 and c=1 in TKD. For u=e_q1 and v=e_p1, the transformed vectors are

$$
Mu=(2,0,0,1),\qquad Mv=(0,1,1,0)
$$

in their respective position and momentum units. The two canonical-plane contributions are 2 Js and −1 Js. Their sum is the original 1 Js.

Thus a general symplectic map need not preserve each projected pair area separately. The complete two-form, evaluated on every pair of tangents, is the invariant. Two displayed projections are an aid to reading the calculation; neither projection alone proves the four-dimensional statement.

## Build a volume-preserving counterexample

Consider the invertible comparison scaling

$$
D_{\mathrm{v}}=\operatorname{diag}(a,b,1/b,1/a).
$$

Its determinant is one, but its canonical-pair factors are a/b and b/a. At a=2,b=1, the first basis-pair area becomes 2 Js and the second becomes 0.5 Js. Hence the map is not symplectic. The equal-scale case a=b is an important exception: it reduces to reciprocal canonical scaling.

The fourth family is TKD_v. Left composition by the canonical shears does not repair the defect: its pullback form is D_v^TJD_v. Thus a=b remains the exact condition for this family's symplecticity, independently of h and c.

In four dimensions the volume form is omega wedge omega divided by two. With our coordinate order it equals minus dq1 wedge dq2 wedge dp1 wedge dp2; its unit is Js². The minus sign fixes orientation and does not describe a negative physical volume. The two pair factors above multiply to one, preserving this top form while changing omega itself. The code checks the top-form coefficient using the four-by-four Pfaffian as well as the determinant.

## A passing probe can hide a failed transformation

For the comparison map, the pure q1-q2 plane still has zero symplectic area before and after transformation. A single zero-area probe therefore passes even when the full map fails.

A different plane exposes the failure. Choose u=(1,1,0,0) and v=(0,0,1,−1). Its original contributions are 1 Js and −1 Js, summing to zero. With a=2,b=1, the transformed sum is 2−0.5=1.5 Js. This plane is two-dimensional and initially Lagrangian, but its image is not.

A pair of parallel vectors is weaker still. The dependent probe has rank one and zero pairing under every linear map. Its area ratio is undefined, because zero divided by zero carries no information. The interface must retain that unavailable ratio rather than inventing one.

## Relate the boundary integral to oriented action

The canonical one-form is theta=p1 dq1+p2 dq2, with omega=−d theta. For the boundary oriented first along u and then v,

$$
\oint\theta=-\omega(u,v).
$$

For the first basis probe, the original integral is −1 Js; the comparison map with a=2,b=1 gives −2 Js. For the coupled canonical example, the sum stays −1 Js even though the two projected contributions change.

The boundary parameter ranges from 0 to 4, one unit per edge. Moving the query selects partial integrals along that constructed loop. Partial integrals may differ between coordinate representations even when the full loop integral agrees. They are actions in Js, not mechanical work in J, and no physical traversal time or energy conservation is being asserted.

## Connect the linear examples to Hamiltonian geometry

For a smooth Hamiltonian H and a nondegenerate form, a Hamiltonian vector field is defined by inserting it into omega to obtain dH under our sign convention. Closedness then makes its Lie derivative of omega vanish: Cartan's identity reduces the result to d(dH)=0. The conclusion holds wherever the flow exists; it does not assert global-in-time existence for an arbitrary system.

Volume preservation is a consequence, not an equivalent replacement in higher dimensions. For example the linear field (q1,0,0,−p2) has zero divergence, but its flow scales the two canonical pairings oppositely and does not preserve the full form. The finite comparison map illustrates the same distinction.

Local canonical coordinates do not imply that every symplectic manifold has one global Cartesian chart. Global topology, nonlinear maps, reduction and long-time numerical integration require further arguments. This lesson establishes the definitions and a fully checked family of coupled four-dimensional linear maps.

## Exercises with explanations

1. Evaluate omega on e_q1 and e_p1. **Solution:** The first determinant is 1 Js and the second zero. Exchanging the vectors gives −1 Js, while pairing a vector with itself gives zero.
2. Explain why determinant one works for a single canonical pair. **Solution:** For a two-by-two matrix, M^TJM=(det M)J. That identity does not reduce the four-by-four test to one scalar.
3. Compute the comparison-map factors for a=2,b=1. **Solution:** They are 2 and 0.5. Their product preserves the top volume form, but neither reproduces the unit basis pairing required by the full form.
4. Can a canonical map change the area of a displayed pair projection? **Solution:** Yes. The coupled example gives 2 Js and −1 Js in the two planes while preserving the total 1 Js.
5. Why does the dependent probe not certify a map? **Solution:** Its tangent span has dimension one, so every alternating pairing vanishes. The full matrix identity still needs to be checked.
6. What is the sign and unit of the closed action on the first basis probe? **Solution:** With the stated u-then-v orientation and theta=p dq it is −1 Js. Reversing orientation changes the sign; it is not an energy in joules.

## Research connection and sources

A learned latent transformation should state whether it preserves volume, canonical brackets or the full symplectic form. Check coupled directions and the full derivative, not just selected phase projections or a determinant loss. A small sampled numerical error and an exact structural identity are different kinds of evidence.

Background definitions and geometric context are available in Ana Cannas da Silva's [Symplectic Geometry, sections 1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0505366v1) and her author-hosted [Lectures on Symplectic Geometry](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~acannas/Papers/lsg.pdf). The controlled map families, unit bookkeeping, action probes and numerical examples here are derived directly and checked with independent symbolic generators, pullbacks and path integrals.
